A realistic cleaning routine for a large family

By Sam Carless, mum of five5 October 20266 minute read

With five children in the house, my home never stays looking like a show home for long. Fingerprints appear on freshly cleaned glass, crumbs follow every snack, and the washing basket seems to refill itself overnight.

A big family doesn't need more cleaning. It needs a smarter routine: a few short resets every day, one bigger job each day of the week, and everyone pitching in. This is the routine I'd suggest for any busy household, whether you have three children or seven.

The aim is a home that's clean enough, most of the time. A routine you can keep up on a tired Tuesday beats a spotless Saturday that leaves you exhausted.

Four rules that make big-family cleaning manageable

  1. Little and often. Three short resets a day stop mess snowballing into a whole-day job.
  2. One bigger job a day. Spreading the jobs across the week means no single day takes over.
  3. Everyone helps. Even a toddler can put toys back in a basket. There's a list of jobs by age further down.
  4. Keep supplies where the mess happens. A spray and a cloth in the kitchen and in each bathroom means a 30-second wipe actually happens.

Your daily resets

Set a timer and stop when it goes off. Done is better than perfect.

Morning reset 10 minutes

  • Breakfast dishes into the dishwasher, or into the sink to soak
  • Wipe the table and kitchen worktops
  • Quick wipe of the bathroom sink and taps
  • Put one load of washing on

After-school reset 10 minutes

  • Shoes, coats and bags back in their places
  • Lunchboxes emptied and washed
  • Toys back in their baskets before tea

Evening reset 15 minutes

  • Clear the kitchen, wipe the worktops and hob, and set the dishwasher going
  • Move the washing to the dryer or airer
  • Sweep or vacuum the kitchen floor, the room that catches the most crumbs
  • Quick living room tidy: cushions, toys and cups
  • Lay out tomorrow's uniforms and school bags

One job a day: a weekly plan

On top of the daily resets, give each day one bigger job of 20 to 30 minutes.

DayJob
MondayBathrooms: toilets, sinks, showers and mirrors
TuesdayKitchen: cupboard fronts, appliances and the microwave
WednesdayFloors: vacuum and mop downstairs
ThursdayBedrooms: change the bedding, tidy and vacuum
FridayFridge clear-out before the weekly food shop
SaturdayFamily power hour: everyone does their jobs at the same time
SundaySunday reset: put the laundry away, fresh towels out, plan the week

If a day goes wrong, skip it rather than doubling up tomorrow. It will come round again next week.

Laundry for a big family

Laundry is the job that never ends in a big household. These habits keep it moving:

  • One load a day, every day, so it never piles up into a mountain
  • A basket for each person, so sorting happens as you fold
  • Older children put away their own clothes, once they're about six
  • A pot for odd socks, matched once a week rather than every day

Cleaning jobs children can do at every age

Children who help from a young age grow up thinking it's normal. Give each child the same jobs for a few weeks so they become habits.

AgeJobs they can do
2 to 4Put toys in baskets, put dirty clothes in the wash basket, wipe up spills with a damp cloth
5 to 7Make their bed, set and clear the table, match socks, feed pets
8 to 11Load and empty the dishwasher, vacuum their room, fold and put away their washing
12 and overClean a bathroom sink and toilet, change their bed, cook a simple meal, take the bins out

Keep cleaning products out of reach of young children and let them help with warm water and a cloth instead. Show older children how to use sprays safely, and never mix cleaning products, especially anything containing bleach.

Monthly jobs, one a week

These are the jobs that get forgotten. Rotate one each week so they're all done once a month.

  • Week 1: wipe skirting boards, door handles and light switches
  • Week 2: clean the oven and the inside of the microwave
  • Week 3: wash the bins and descale the kettle
  • Week 4: vacuum under the sofas and beds, and wash the cushion covers

When it all falls apart

Sickness bugs, school holidays and busy weeks will knock any routine. When that happens, go back to the basics: the evening kitchen reset, one load of washing a day, and nothing else. Once the house feels manageable again, pick the weekly plan back up on whatever day it is.

Quick answers

How long should cleaning take each day with a big family?

Around 35 minutes of short resets spread across the day, plus 20 to 30 minutes for the day's bigger job. Short bursts are much easier to fit around school runs than one long session.

How do I get my children to help without a battle?

Keep the jobs the same for a few weeks, put them on a simple chart where everyone can see it, and do them together at the same time. Music helps more than you'd think.

Which cleaning products does a big family actually need?

Fewer than you might expect. A multi-purpose spray, a bathroom cleaner, washing-up liquid, hand wash, a floor cleaner and a few good microfibre cloths cover most everyday jobs.
